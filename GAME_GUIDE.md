# The Chronicles of Rayhaven

*A Grand Strategy Game of Conquest, Honor, and Glory*

---

## Prologue

In the mystical lands of Rayhaven, great houses vie for dominion over a realm of untold riches. Each noble house commands loyal subjects—brave souls who earn their standing through merit and deed. From humble peasants to mighty kings, all serve their house in the eternal struggle for supremacy.

Will your house rise to glory, or crumble beneath the boots of your rivals?

---

## The Realm

Rayhaven is a vast hexagonal battlefield where up to **ten noble houses** compete for land, resources, and honor. The realm is divided into countless hexes, each a precious plot of territory waiting to be claimed.

Your house's strength is measured not merely in gold, but in:
- **Territories** under your banner
- **Fortifications** defending your realm
- **Armies** projecting your power
- **The valor** of your subjects

---

## Thy Subjects

Each house commands a roster of loyal subjects—students of war and commerce who earn **Merit Points** through their deeds beyond the battlefield. These points determine their rank and prowess in combat.

### The Ranks of Nobility

| Rank | Merit Required | Vitality | Strength |
|------|----------------|----------|----------|
| Peasant | 0 - 99 | 20 | 10 |
| Farmer | 100 - 999 | 30 | 15 |
| Merchant | 1,000 - 1,999 | 40 | 20 |
| Knight | 2,000 - 4,999 | 60 | 30 |
| Grand Knight | 5,000 - 7,999 | 80 | 45 |
| Baron | 8,000 - 9,999 | 100 | 60 |
| King | 10,000+ | 150 | 80 |

*A subject's rank reflects their experience and training. Higher ranks strike harder and endure longer in battle.*

## Claiming the Land

To build your empire, you must first claim territory. Land painted in your house colors belongs to you—defend it jealously, for rivals covet every hex.

**To claim land:** Enter Land Mode and click upon unclaimed hexes. The territory shall bear your colors forevermore (or until wrested from your grasp).

## Structures of Power

### The Town (1,000 gold)

A town is the foundation of civilization. Requiring **seven contiguous hexes** of your territory, a town provides:
- A place of **refuge** where fallen subjects may be revived
- **Sanctuary** where subjects may rest and fully recover their vitality
- The prerequisite for constructing a mighty castle

*To house a subject: Hold Shift and click upon the town, then select a subject to shelter within its walls.*

### The Castle (5,000 gold)

The crown jewel of any kingdom. A castle requires:
- An existing town at its heart
- **Nineteen contiguous hexes** of territory (the town's seven plus twelve more)
- Tremendous investment of resources

Castles are nigh impregnable, requiring **four to five strikes from a King** to fell. They provide the same sanctuary as towns, but their grandeur reflects the might of your house.

## Instruments of War

### Walls (25 gold)
*"A wise lord builds walls before his enemies build armies."*

Walls are erected upon the **edges** between hexes, creating barriers against invasion. Cheap and quickly rebuilt, they form the backbone of any defensive strategy.

### Armies (100 gold)
*"Gold wins wars, but soldiers win battles."*

Armies are stationed upon your territory, projecting power and threatening rival lands. They cannot attack on their own but serve as formidable obstacles to enemy advances.

### Ships (75 gold)
*"He who commands the waters commands the realm."*

Ships may sail upon any hex, carrying a single passenger across the realm. A ship with a warrior aboard may attack using that warrior's strength. Beware—should the ship sink, its passenger drowns with it.

*To board: Hold Shift and click the ship, then select a subject to embark.*

### Bridges (50 gold)
*"Where there is no path, build one."*

Bridges span difficult terrain, rotating to connect different directions. Right-click to rotate before placement.

## The Art of War

Combat in Rayhaven follows ancient traditions:

### Initiating Combat
1. Enter **Battle Mode** (press X)
2. Select your **attacker** (a subject on the field, or a crewed ship)
3. Select your **target** (enemy subject, structure, or vessel)

### Rules of Engagement

- **Subject vs Subject:** Both combatants strike simultaneously. The mightier warrior often prevails, but even a peasant's blade can wound a king.

- **Subject vs Structure:** Only the structure takes damage. Walls do not fight back—but they delay your advance.

- **Crewed Ships:** Attack with the strength of their passenger.

- **Friendly Fire:** Regrettably permitted. Command your forces wisely.

### Destruction & Death

When a structure falls, its cost is forever lost to your treasury. When a subject falls:
- They are marked as **fallen** (not removed)
- They may be **revived** at a friendly town or castle
- Revival costs **10 gold** and restores full vitality

## The Treasury

Your house's wealth is measured in two ways:

### Merit Points
The sum of all your subjects' individual merit—earned through deeds beyond the game. **This total is never spent**; it represents your house's true worth.

### Available Gold
Your spending power, calculated as:

```
Available Gold = Merit Points
               - Cost of Standing Structures
               - Cost of Lost Structures
               - Cost of Revivals
```

*Every wall built, every castle lost, every fallen soldier revived—all drain your coffers. Spend wisely.*

### Structure Costs

| Structure | Cost | Durability |
|-----------|------|------------|
| Wall | 25 | 50 |
| Bridge | 50 | 40 |
| Ship | 75 | 60 |
| Army | 100 | 100 |
| Town | 1,000 | 120 |
| Castle | 5,000 | 350 |

## Strategies for Victory

*Wisdom passed down from the great lords of old:*

1. **Expand early, fortify often.** Land unclaimed is land lost to rivals.

2. **Walls are cheap; subjects are not.** Let your fortifications absorb enemy blows.

3. **Towns before castles.** A network of towns provides more revival points than a single grand castle.

4. **Guard your ships.** A drowned knight is a tragedy; a lost king, a catastrophe.

5. **Track your losses.** Every destroyed structure drains your treasury. Sometimes retreat preserves more than it surrenders.

6. **Strength in numbers.** Even peasants can fell a king if they strike together.
